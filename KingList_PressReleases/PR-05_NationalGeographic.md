## HEADLINE: Did a Comet Wipe Out the Mammoths? New Evidence From 72 Sites Across the Globe
### FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE — AUGUST 2026

---

**WASHINGTON, DC** — A groundbreaking study published today provides the most comprehensive evidence yet that a fragmented comet struck Earth 12,800 years ago, triggering the extinction of mammoths, mastodons, saber-toothed cats, and giant ground sloths across North America.

The research, spanning 72 excavation sites on six continents, reveals a layer of impact debris containing hexagonal nanodiamonds (lonsdaleite) that only form under pressures greater than 50 GPa — achievable only by cosmic impact. The debris also includes high-temperature melt-glass (>2,200°C), microscopic spherules, and a charcoal layer indicating continent-scale wildfires.

"What we're looking at is the forensic evidence of a cosmic impact that melted glaciers, triggered mega-floods, and burned an area the size of South America," said Jason Brodsky, Creative Director of Carrington Storm Motors.

**The Connection to Ancient Knowledge:**
The study draws a direct line from this ancient catastrophe to the Sumerian King List — a 4,100-year-old clay document that records eight "antediluvian" kings ruling for a total of 241,200 years before a great flood. The research team interprets this not as mythology but as a compressed astronomical record.

**The 2032 Warning:**
Using data from the Vera C. Rubin Observatory, the researchers identify the Taurid Resonant Swarm — debris from the same comet family — as an ongoing threat. Earth's next deep passage: November 1-8, 2032.

**Open-Source Response:**
The study is accompanied by 68 open-source fabrication documents for shelters, power systems, and communication networks designed to withstand future impacts.
