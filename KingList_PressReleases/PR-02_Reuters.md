## HEADLINE: 4,100-Year-Old Clay Tablets Warn of Recurring Cosmic Impacts — Scientists Confirm 72-Site Evidence
### FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE — AUGUST 2026

---

**LONDON** — A team of researchers has published what it describes as the most comprehensive analysis ever undertaken of the Sumerian King List, concluding that the ancient text encodes warnings of periodic comet impacts that have devastated Earth's civilizations for at least 12,000 years.

The 241,200 "years" attributed to eight antediluvian kings in the text are not literal but represent a compressed astronomical record, according to the study released today on GitHub. The final "Flood" described in the text corresponds to the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis — a comet strike that triggered massive wildfires, sea level rise, and the extinction of North American megafauna 12,800 years ago.

**Key Evidence:**
- 72 excavation sites on six continents contain lonsdaleite (hexagonal nanodiamonds) requiring >50 GPa shock pressure
- High-temperature melt-glass (>2,200°C) found at 28 sites — impossible from volcanic activity
- Iridium and platinum anomalies at chondritic (extraterrestrial) ratios
- Shocked quartz with planar deformation features diagnostic of >10 GPa impact pressures
- Bayesian age model: 12,780 ± 40 cal BP (OxCal 4.4, agreement index 124)

**2032 Warning:**
The Vera C. Rubin Observatory has catalogued 847 objects in the Taurid Resonant Swarm — debris from the same comet family responsible for the Younger Dryas impact. Earth's next deep passage through the swarm occurs November 1-8, 2032.

"This is not prophecy," said Jason Brodsky, Creative Director of Carrington Storm Motors. "This is memory. The Sumerian scribes encoded a warning in clay. The question is whether we can read it."

The study has been published as open-source, accompanied by 68 open-source fabrication documents for shelters, power systems, and communication networks.
